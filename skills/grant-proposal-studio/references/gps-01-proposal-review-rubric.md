# Proposal Review Rubric

> General guidance for US nonprofits as of October 2026, not legal, tax, or accounting advice. Rules elsewhere differ. Review with qualified advisors before relying on it.

Use this rubric to score a draft proposal the way an experienced foundation program officer would. Score each criterion 1 to 5, apply the weight, and report a weighted total out of 100. Always explain each score with evidence from the draft and give the single most valuable fix. When the funder publishes its own criteria and weights, score against those instead.

| Criterion | Weight | What reviewers look for |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Need and evidence | 20 | Specific, local, recent data; names who is affected and why now; cites sources |
| 2. Funder alignment | 15 | Mirrors the funder's stated priorities, population, and geography in their own language |
| 3. Program design and logic | 20 | Clear activities linked to outcomes; realistic dosage, timeline, and staffing; a simple logic model |
| 4. Outcomes and evaluation | 15 | SMART objectives; named measures, data sources, collection timing, and who reports |
| 5. Organizational capacity | 10 | Track record with numbers, qualified staff, partners, governance, financial health |
| 6. Budget and sustainability | 10 | Budget matches narrative; costs are reasonable and allowable; credible plan after the grant ends |
| 7. Clarity and compliance | 10 | Follows every instruction (limits, format, attachments); plain language; no jargon |

## Score level descriptors

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 5 Exceptional | Fully meets every reviewer expectation with specific evidence; a reviewer would quote it |
| 4 Strong | Meets expectations with minor gaps that do not weaken the case |
| 3 Adequate | Covers the criterion but is generic, partly unsupported, or misses a requirement |
| 2 Weak | Significant gaps, vague claims, or a mismatch with the funder |
| 1 Missing | Absent or contradicts the funder's requirements |

## Weighted total

Weighted total = sum of (score / 5 x weight). 85 to 100 is competitive; 70 to 84 is fundable with revisions; below 70 needs significant work before submission. These bands are this tool's rules of thumb, not a funder standard; score against the funder's published criteria and weights when they exist.

## Red flags that cost points

- Statistics with no source, or older than five years (this tool's rule of thumb, not a funder standard; score against the funder's published criteria when they exist).
- Outcomes that are really outputs (for example, 'serve 200 youth' instead of 'increase on-time graduation by 10 points').
- Budget lines that never appear in the narrative, or narrative activities with no budget.
- Exceeding page or word limits, or missing a required attachment.
- Generic language that could describe any organization.
