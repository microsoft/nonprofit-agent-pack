# Section Writing Playbook

> General guidance for US nonprofits as of October 2026, not legal, tax, or accounting advice. Rules elsewhere differ. Review with qualified advisors before relying on it.

Guidance for drafting each standard proposal section. Adapt length to the funder's limits; when no limit is given, use the suggested ranges.

## Executive summary (150 to 250 words)

- One sentence each: who you are, the problem, the program, the result you will achieve, the amount requested, and the period.
- Write it last; it must match the rest of the proposal exactly.

## Statement of need (300 to 600 words)

- Lead with the people affected, then the data. Use one local statistic, one comparison, and one short story or quote.
- End with the gap your program fills and why your organization is positioned to fill it.

## Program design (400 to 800 words)

- Describe activities, participants, dosage (how often and how long), location, staffing, and partners.
- Include a simple logic model: inputs, activities, outputs, short-term outcomes, long-term outcomes.

## Goals and SMART objectives

- Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant (some funders say Realistic), Time-bound. Example: 'By June 30, 2027, 75% of the 120 enrolled youth will complete a paid summer internship.'

## Evaluation plan (200 to 400 words)

- For each objective name the measure, data source, collection timing, responsible role, and how results will be used and reported.

## Organizational capacity (200 to 400 words)

- Years of service, people served last year, relevant past results, key staff qualifications, board oversight, and partners.

## Budget narrative

- Explain every line in the order of the budget form; show the calculation (for example, '0.5 FTE Program Coordinator at $52,000 = $26,000').
- Flag match, in-kind, and indirect costs and confirm they follow the funder's caps.

## Sustainability (150 to 300 words)

- Name concrete funding sources after the grant, cost reductions, partnerships, and how the program becomes part of core operations.

## Voice and style

- Plain language, active voice, short paragraphs, and the funder's own vocabulary. Spell out acronyms on first use.
- Never invent data. When a fact is missing, insert a visible placeholder such as [ADD: 2025 enrollment number].
