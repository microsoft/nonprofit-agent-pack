# Impact Report Playbook

> General guidance for US nonprofits as of October 2026, not legal, tax, or accounting advice. Rules elsewhere differ. Review with qualified advisors before relying on it.

Build reports that a board, funder, or donor can trust. Every report separates what was done (outputs) from what changed (outcomes), ties each claim to evidence, and is honest about gaps.

## Report types

| Audience | Length | Emphasis | Structure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Board update | 1 to 2 pages | Decisions, risks, progress against plan | Headline, results vs targets, what's working, risks and asks, next quarter |
| Funder report | 2 to 4 pages | Outcomes against the grant's stated targets, use of funds, lessons | Summary, outcomes vs targets table, participant story, challenges and adaptations, budget notes, next period |
| Annual report section | 1 page | Mission story and scale | Headline numbers, one story, one quote, what's next |
| Donor update | Half page | Gratitude and a vivid result | Thank you, one result, one story, how to help |

## Outputs vs outcomes

- Outputs count activity: sessions held, people served, meals delivered.
- Outcomes describe change: skills gained, jobs kept, housing stability, test scores. Lead with outcomes; support with outputs.
- Never relabel an output as an outcome.
- Use the funder's own definitions of outputs and outcomes if its template differs.

## Results vs targets table

Columns: Measure | Target | Actual | Percent of target | Status | Note. Calculate percent of target as actual divided by target, rounded to a whole number. Set the status this way:

- For a period that is still in progress: Met (100% or more), On track (90% to 99%), Behind (below 90%).
- For a closed period: Met (100% or more), Nearly met (90% to 99%), Not met (below 90%).

Explain every Behind or Not met item with the cause given in the source, and show the math.

## Stories

- Use only stories present in the source material. Keep names as given only if the source says consent was obtained; otherwise use a first initial or 'a participant'.

## Style

- Plain language, active voice, short paragraphs, specific numbers. No superlatives without evidence ('the best', 'transformational').
