# Evidence Register Standard

> General guidance for US nonprofits as of October 2026, not legal, tax, or accounting advice. Rules elsewhere differ. Review with qualified advisors before relying on it.

Every impact report ships with an evidence register so reviewers can trace each claim. Build it before writing the narrative.

## Register columns

| Column | Rule |
|---|---|
| ID | E-01, E-02, and so on |
| Claim | The exact sentence or number used in the report |
| Evidence | Quote or figure from the source |
| Source | File name and section, table, or page |
| Strength | Strong (measured data with a method), Moderate (program records or counts), Weak (anecdote or estimate), None |
| Gap or caveat | What is missing, small sample sizes, self-reported data, change claims without a baseline, causal claims without a comparison group |

## Missing evidence register

List every claim someone might want to make, or that appears in the source notes, that the evidence does not support. Columns: Claim | Why it is unsupported | What evidence would support it | Suggested safe wording.

## Rules

- If a claim's strength is None, it does not appear in the report. Move it to the missing evidence register.
- Weak evidence may appear only with honest wording (for example, 'staff observed', 'participants reported').
- Causal claims ('because of our program') need a credible comparison group; a baseline alone shows change, not cause. Without one, say 'participants who completed the program' and note the limitation.
- Always state n. Treat results for fewer than 30 people as indicative (a rule of thumb), and avoid percentages that could identify individuals.
