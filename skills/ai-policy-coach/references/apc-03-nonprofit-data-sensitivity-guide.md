# Nonprofit Data Sensitivity Guide

> General guidance for US nonprofits as of October 2026, not legal, tax, or accounting advice. Rules elsewhere differ. Review with qualified advisors before relying on it.

Classify the organization's data so the policy and agent rules match real risk.

| Level | Examples | AI handling |
|---|---|---|
| Public | Annual reports, website content, published research | Any approved tool |
| Internal | Meeting notes, plans, internal procedures | Enterprise tools signed in with work accounts |
| Confidential | Donor records, staff records, financial details, contracts | Enterprise tools only; never public AI; share agents only with authorized groups |
| Highly sensitive | Client case files, health information, information about minors, survivors of violence, immigration status, legal matters | Only approved, reviewed agents with access controls; consider excluding from AI until controls are in place |

Information about minors is always Highly sensitive.

Some of this information may also be protected by law: for example, in the United States, HIPAA (if your organization is a covered entity or business associate), FERPA (for student education records), or COPPA (for online services directed to children under 13); or by privacy laws in other countries, contracts, or grant terms. This guide isn't legal advice, so confirm which rules apply with your legal or privacy advisor.

## Questions that raise the risk level

- Does the work involve minors, health, legal status, or safety? Does an AI output affect someone's access to services? Is the content shared with the public or funders?
