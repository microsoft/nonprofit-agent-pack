# AI Acceptable Use Policy: Clause Library

> General guidance for US nonprofits as of October 2026, not legal, tax, or accounting advice. Rules elsewhere differ. Review with qualified advisors before relying on it.

Assemble a policy by choosing the variant that matches the organization's risk posture: Cautious, Balanced, or Progressive. Keep the final policy to about two pages, in plain language, with a review date.

## 1. Purpose and scope

This policy explains how [Organization] staff, volunteers, and contractors may use artificial intelligence (AI) tools, including Microsoft Copilot (formerly Microsoft 365 Copilot), Microsoft Copilot Chat (formerly Microsoft 365 Copilot Chat), Copilot Studio agents, and public AI services, to support our mission while protecting the people we serve.

## 2. Approved tools

- Cautious: only organization-approved tools signed in with work accounts (for example, Microsoft Copilot Chat with enterprise data protection). Public consumer AI tools are not allowed for work content.
- Balanced: approved enterprise tools for work content; Highly sensitive data only in tools approved for it (for example, with web search turned off); public AI tools only for non-sensitive, public information.
- Progressive: approved enterprise tools by default; teams may pilot new tools after a short risk review by the AI lead.

## 3. Data rules

- Never enter into public AI tools: client or participant records, health information, information about minors, donor financial details, immigration status, case notes, passwords, or anything marked Confidential.
- In approved enterprise tools, follow existing access rules: AI does not change who is allowed to see information.

## 4. Human review

- A person is accountable for every AI-assisted output. Review facts, numbers, names, and tone before sharing externally.
- Cautious and Balanced: AI may not make or communicate decisions about eligibility, services, employment, or discipline. Progressive: AI may draft such communications, but a named staff member must review and send them.
- Clients and the public can contact [contact] to raise concerns about our use of AI or to ask a person to review an AI-assisted decision or response.

## 5. Transparency

- Disclose AI assistance when content is published, when AI interacts directly with clients or the public, or when a funder requires it.
- Label AI-generated images, audio, and video.

## 6. Building agents

- Cautious: only IT may build and share agents. Balanced: trained makers may build agents in a designated environment; IT reviews before sharing beyond a team. Progressive: makers may build and share within their department; agents that use sensitive data or act on behalf of users require review.

## 7. Fairness, accessibility, and harm

- Check outputs for bias, stereotypes, and inaccessible language. Outputs must meet [Organization]'s accessibility standards, not just plain language. Report harmful or incorrect outputs to the AI lead.

## 8. Training and support

- All users complete a 30-minute AI basics session before access is turned on, or within 30 days. Name the AI lead and how to get help.

## 9. Incidents

- If sensitive information is entered into an unapproved tool, report it to [privacy contact] the same day.

## 10. Review

- This policy is reviewed every 6 months (Cautious and Balanced) or every quarter (Progressive), because AI tools change quickly. This policy isn't legal advice; review local law.

## 11. Third-party tools and intellectual property

- Check the terms of any third-party AI tool before using it for work, including how it uses your data. Don't paste others' copyrighted material or confidential partner data into AI tools. Review AI output for copyrighted content before you publish it.

## 12. Records and retention

- AI prompts and outputs may be records. Follow [Organization]'s retention policy.

## 13. Children and youth

- Never put information about minors in public AI tools. Any AI that interacts with youth needs leadership and legal review before it launches.
